# EXP-20260825 — Six-Model Replication of the Kana Orthography Intervention

## Result in one sentence

On the frozen 382-item primary analysis set, five of six models showed a positive target-versus-unrelated-control effect on the correct–`wrong1` margin; the fixed six-model mean was `+0.136`, with a paired bootstrap 95% confidence interval of `[+0.100, +0.173]`. Final four-option accuracy remained strongly model-dependent.

## Design

- Frozen material: 450 items; SHA-256 `fa19564838ab4d86f8ecda1a08daf9e792e3d576734138927f2b569ef933b4c9`.
- Primary analysis: 382 items after excluding 35 development items and weak unrelated-control proxies.
- Conditions: original source, target word converted to kana, and an unrelated kanji-bearing component converted to kana.
- Additional sensitivity condition: a semantically related control was available for 411/450 items.
- Score: candidate-only mean NLL, used to avoid directly averaging source-token changes into the outcome.

```text
margin = NLL(wrong1) - NLL(correct)
orthography_effect = margin(target_kana) - margin(unrelated_control_kana)
```

Larger margins favor `correct` over `wrong1`. A positive effect means that removing visible target-word kanji improves the correct-versus-`wrong1` preference relative to the generic kana-rewrite control. It does not imply higher four-option accuracy.

The five new model runs reused the frozen materials, revisions, analysis set, and metric before their formal results were inspected. The existing Qwen result was incorporated only in the final six-model synthesis. See the [replication preregistration](EXP-20260825-orthography-five-model-replication-preregistered.md).

## Primary continuous effect

| Model | Mean effect | Paired bootstrap 95% CI | Positive / negative items |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-1M | +0.121 | [+0.047, +0.198] | 217 / 165 |
| llm-jp-3-7.2b-instruct3 | −0.037 | [−0.076, +0.003] | 186 / 196 |
| Llama-3-ELYZA-JP-8B | +0.076 | [+0.030, +0.122] | 234 / 148 |
| Baichuan2-7B-Base | +0.311 | [+0.260, +0.363] | 291 / 91 |
| Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 | +0.225 | [+0.173, +0.279] | 253 / 129 |
| Gemma-7B | +0.117 | [+0.062, +0.172] | 227 / 155 |

Five intervals are entirely above zero. The llm-jp mean is slightly negative, but its interval crosses zero and does not establish a reliable negative effect.

The preregistered fixed-set summary first averages the six model effects within each item and then bootstraps the 382 items 10,000 times:

```text
mean effect = +0.1359
95% CI = [+0.1002, +0.1726]
positive / negative items = 252 / 130
```

This procedure does not treat the `6 × 382` model–item rows as independent. It describes the fixed model set and should not be interpreted as a random sample from all language models.

## Negative-control check

| Model | Target − original | Unrelated control − original | Target − control |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-1M | +0.138 | +0.017 | +0.121 |
| llm-jp-3-7.2b-instruct3 | −0.028 | +0.008 | −0.037 |
| Llama-3-ELYZA-JP-8B | +0.057 | −0.019 | +0.076 |
| Baichuan2-7B-Base | +0.308 | −0.004 | +0.311 |
| Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 | +0.253 | +0.027 | +0.225 |
| Gemma-7B | +0.118 | +0.001 | +0.117 |
| Six-model mean | **+0.141** | **+0.005** | **+0.136** |

The unrelated rewrite is close to zero in every model, whereas target-word rewriting produces a larger, model-dependent change. This makes a purely generic sentence-rewrite explanation less plausible.

The controls were generated by source-side rules and did not receive independent bilingual validation. Their near-zero aggregate behavior supports empirical validity as a negative control but does not prove that every individual control is perfectly natural and semantically independent.

## Four-option accuracy and error redistribution

| Model | Original correct | Target-kana correct | Δ correct | Original `wrong1` | Target-kana `wrong1` | Δ `wrong1` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-1M | 265 | 250 | −15 | 94 | 88 | −6 |
| llm-jp-3-7.2b-instruct3 | 197 | 182 | −15 | 140 | 146 | +6 |
| Llama-3-ELYZA-JP-8B | 182 | 176 | −6 | 174 | 164 | −10 |
| Baichuan2-7B-Base | 227 | 270 | **+43** | 131 | 71 | **−60** |
| Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 | 167 | 203 | **+36** | 164 | 106 | **−58** |
| Gemma-7B | 223 | 222 | −1 | 133 | 100 | −33 |

Three patterns are visible:

1. Baichuan and Mistral improve in continuous margin, `wrong1` count, and final accuracy.
2. Gemma substantially reduces `wrong1`, but errors move to `wrong2` and `wrong3`, leaving accuracy almost unchanged.
3. Qwen and ELYZA improve the correct-versus-`wrong1` margin while losing accuracy through other candidates; llm-jp does not reproduce the positive margin effect.

Removing visible orthography is therefore neither monotonically beneficial nor monotonically harmful.

## Cross-model heterogeneity

The number of models with a positive effect per item was:

| Positive models | Items |
|---:|---:|
| 0 | 9 |
| 1 | 33 |
| 2 | 53 |
| 3 | 74 |
| 4 | 76 |

At least four models had a positive effect on 213/382 items. Fifty-eight items were positive for all six models, while nine were negative for all six. Pairwise item-level effect correlations ranged from 0.215 to 0.455: positive but modest. The results suggest both shared item sensitivity and substantial model-specific behavior.

The absolute correlations between token-count change and the primary effect are at most 0.110. Simple token-count increases therefore do not explain the result. Token identity, vocabulary coverage, and hidden-representation changes remain possible contributors.

Exact-versus-variant analyses are exploratory and heterogeneous. Only Baichuan's diagnostic interval excluded zero, and no multiple-comparison correction was applied. No cross-model exact-form conclusion is claimed.

## Supported claims

- Under the frozen Japanese-to-Chinese materials and candidate-only correct-versus-`wrong1` margin, target-word kana conversion produces a positive fixed-set effect across these six models.
- Five individual models show intervals above zero.
- The unrelated negative control is empirically close to zero on average.
- Final accuracy effects are heterogeneous, motivating selective rather than universal intervention.

## Unsupported stronger claims

- Orthography is the strongest source of error among naturalness, frequency, semantic distance, token length, and label noise.
- Kana conversion generally improves translation accuracy.
- The model internally implements a specific process in which it sees kanji and skips semantic checking.
- The effect generalizes without qualification to other directions, datasets, scales, or open-ended generation.
- The automatically generated controls constitute a human-validated gold standard.

## Implication for reliability-aware routing

The experiment establishes two prerequisites for a selective system:

1. visible orthography can change model preference and is therefore a meaningful signal to model;
2. the same intervention can help, do little, or hurt depending on the item and model.

A follow-up system should predict whether a form-derived proposal is reliable, then choose among retaining it, triggering semantic verification, retrieving external evidence, or abstaining. Evaluation should use held-out lexical splits and jointly report accuracy, risk–coverage, calibration, and computational cost.
